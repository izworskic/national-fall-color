How we make the call

1. We compare each stop with its usual fall timing

Every elevation turns on a different schedule. We compare each Parkway stop with the time of year it usually makes its strongest fall change instead of pretending the whole road peaks at once.

2. We check current leaf reports

Recent colored-leaf reports help tell us whether this season looks early, late or about normal. We never turn a few reports into a fake “percent peak.”

3. We check weather and dryness

Hard freezes, strong wind and dry conditions can shorten the show or dull the color, so we flag those separately from the timing outlook.

4. We check whether you can actually get there

A great color window is useless if the Parkway is closed. We flag clearly reported NPS closures and still tell you to verify the official road page before leaving.

Why the map moves: The Parkway runs nearly 470 miles and climbs through huge elevation changes. Higher ridges usually turn first, then the better color tends to move lower as the season goes on. NPS fall color guidance.
Before you leave: Check official Blue Ridge Parkway road status. Weather, construction and storm recovery can close sections even when foliage timing is favorable.

Blue Ridge fall color questions

When is peak color on the Blue Ridge Parkway?

There is not one peak day for all 469 miles. Use the map to see which part of the Parkway has the best color window now, then switch to 7 days from now to see where it may move next.

Why can Asheville look green while nearby ridges are turning?

Elevation can create a large temperature difference over a short drive. High ridges can enter the transition while lower valleys are still earlier in the season.

Are the map pin colors live camera readings?

No. They are a timing outlook for each Parkway stop. Use the live-camera section for the visual check before you drive. We do not invent a fake percent-peak number.

How should I use this for a weekend drive?

Pick the area you can reach, compare Today with 7 days from now, tap the map pins, then check a live camera and NPS road status before you leave.
